### Decentralized Data Ecosystems and Interface Heterogeneity
{:#related-work-ecosystems}

Decentralized data ecosystems consist of self-governed data stores, each individually positioned in the CAP space
and exposed through heterogeneous interfaces [](cite:cites phd-symposium).
Unlike distributed databases under centralized management, they have no unifying mission.

Our goal is to develop a CRDT for RDF that can work across the heterogeneous ecosystem depending only on
unifying aspects of the ecosystem.
To this end, our solution to offline first is the use of a state based CRDT using native RDF,
meaning all required information is included in the state of the data itself, and the data is RDF.
With this choice, the self-governed datastore does not need any special functionality to support the CRDT.
Only requiring that our communication channel eventually sends the updated state to every replication.

### OR-Set CRDTs, SU-Set and Yjs

[Ibáñez et al.](cite:cites rdf-crdt) introduced the SU-Set, an OR-Set construction for RDF graphs
that maps all SPARQL Update 1.1 insert/delete operations to OR-Set operations on ground triples,
with blank nodes replaced by skolemized IRIs, and proved convergence with negligible synchronization overhead.
The introduction of [RDF 1.2](cite:cites rdf-1-2), however, opens new possibilities that SU-Set predates:
triple terms allow CRDT bookkeeping to be modelled natively within the RDF dataset itself,
without external structures or side-channels.
A different line of work uses document-level CRDTs rather than RDF-native ones.
[Gruss et al.](cite:cites solid-crdt-yjs) propose a vocabulary for collaborative Linked Data in Solid,
combining Yjs and Automerge with hypermedia controls
so that clients can discover the collaboration interface at runtime.
Their approach achieves fine-grained co-editing and shifts intelligence to the client,
but treats CRDT state as an opaque binary document stored alongside a textual RDF snapshot.
CRDT-unaware clients must be handled as a separate code path,
and the approach does not extend to quad-store-level abstraction within a query engine.
Our approach instead models the CRDT bookkeeping natively in RDF 1.2,
allowing CRDT-aware and CRDT-unaware clients to coexist without friction
and enabling conflict-free writes to be made transparent at the query engine level.
