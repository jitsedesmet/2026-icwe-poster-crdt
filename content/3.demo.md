## Add-wins State-based CRDT-RDF
{:#core}

<!-- https://katex.org/docs/supported.html -->

Let $$ \mathcal{E} $$ be the countably infinit set of _elements_, and let $$ \mathcal{T} $$ be the countably infinite set of _unique tags_.
A state based OR-set $$ S $$ is a subset of $$ \mathcal{E} \times 2^\mathcal{T} \times 2^\mathcal{T} $$,
meaning it is a set of triples $$ (e, addS, removeS) \text{ with } e \in \mathcal{E} \text{, } addS \subset \mathcal{T} \text{ and } removeS \subset \mathcal{T} $$.
Each element $$ e $$ can appear in the set only once:
$$\forall (e, addS, removeS ) \in S . \forall (e', addS', removeS') \in S . e = e' \implies addS = addS' \land removeS = removeS' $$.
We call an element $$ e $$ _present in the set_ when $$ present(e, S) \Leftrightarrow \exists(e', addS, \_) \in S . e = e' \land addS \ne \emptyset $$.
Three operations exist on the set, namely _merge_ ($$\sqcup$$) (commutative, associative, and idempotent), _add_, and _remove_:

<div markdown="1" style="font-size: 0.8em">
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_1 \sqcup S_2 =& \{ ( e, addS, removeS ) \mid \\
&\kern{2em}(\exists (e_1, addS_1, removeS_1) \in S_1 . \exists (e_2, addS_2, removeS_2) \in S_2 . e = e_1 \land e = e_2 \land \\
&\kern{2em}\quad removeS = removeS_1 \cup removeS_2 \land addS = (addS_1 \cup addS_2) \setminus removeS) \\
&\kern{2em}\lor ( (e, addS, removeS) \in S_1 \land \lnot(\exists addS', removeS' . (e, addS', removeS') \in S_2 ) ) \\
&\kern{2em}\lor ( (e, addS, removeS) \in S_2 \land \lnot(\exists addS', removeS' . (e, addS', removeS') \in S_1 ) ) \\
add(e, S) =& \text{ let } t \in \mathcal{T} \text{ be a fresh unique tag. Then} \\
&\begin{cases}
S \setminus \{(e, addS, removeS)\} \cup \{(e, addS \cup \{t\}, removeS)\} & \text{if } \exists\,(e, addS, removeS) \in S \\
S \cup \{(e, \{t\}, \emptyset)\} & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \\
remove(e, S) =& \\
& \begin{cases}
S \setminus \{(e, addS, removeS)\} \cup \{(e, \emptyset, removeS \cup addS)\} & \text{if } \exists\,(e, addS, removeS) \in S \\
S & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
</div>

Practically tying the CRDT to RDF means we instantiate $$ \mathcal{E} $$ as the set of all valid RDF 1.2 triples,
and instantiate $$ \mathcal{T} $$ as all valid [UUDv4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9562.html) tags.
We represent the above CRDT triple using unique identifier for the CRDT-triple, possibly using a blanknode.
The predicates `crdt:tagging`, `crdt:add` and `crdt:delete` are used to represent $$ e $$, a member of $$ addS $$ and a member of $$ deleteS $$ respectivelly.

### Time optimization

The monotonicity property of state based CRDTs make them impractical without optimizations.
Since our use case is bound to the Web, we can perform optimizations by assuming all clients are currently in a synchronized NTP state.
Since NTP goes into panic mode after a drift of more than 1000s seconds is detected, we can practically assume two clients differ at most $$ 2000 \text{s} \approx 33.5 \text{min} $$.
As such, a CRDT could describe that it expects clients to synchronize every $$ d $$ seconds.
This then allows us to optimize both add tags and remove tags:
1. An add tag for element $$ e $$ may be removed if a more recent add tag exists for $$ e $$ and, the tag is seen by every system,
2. A remove tag for element $$ e $$ may be removed if it is seen by every system,
3. The identifying triple (with predicate `crdt:tagging`) may be removed if the subject is not used by any other triple in the store.

We know every ststem has seen a tag when it's added timestamp is older than $$ d + 4000 $$ seconds.
We require this lower bound since a machine (A) starting at a drift of $$ +1000\text{s} $$ may stall for $$ 2000\text{s} $$ and still functlion correctly according to NTP, meanwile a system (B)
may start at a drift of $$ -1000\text{s} $$ and may jump $$ +2000\text{s} $$ and still be correct.
As such the worse case is when A and B sync, then B adds a tag and A freezes for $$ 2000 $$ seconds, since it only needs to sync every $$ d $$ seconds, it will sync at timestamp $$ t_0 + d + 2000 $$.
While A freezes, B jumps 2000 seconds, so at time $$ t_0 + d + 2000 $$, system B will think this is actually $$ t_0 + d + 4000 $$.
